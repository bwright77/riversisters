The South Platte is not a resource. She is a relative.

River Sisters Congreso works to restore balance, health, and respect for the South Platte River as a living relative — through cultural preservation, environmental justice, policy advocacy, and ceremonial practice.

Stand with the River The Personhood Framework

Initiative 01 — River Personhood Framework

Recognition of the South Platte River as a living being with inherent rights

Guided by Indigenous worldviews, the Congreso is advancing recognition of the River's rights — to flow, to be clean, and to regenerate her ecosystems — with a community-led guardianship model shared between Indigenous representatives, local residents, and city partners. Denver is not first; it would join a growing lineage of river personhood across the world, and could become the model for how a city does it in partnership with its communities.

2017

Whanganui River

Aotearoa / New Zealand — legal personhood through Te Awa Tupua.

2019

Klamath River

Yurok Tribe — first river granted personhood by a North American Indigenous government.

2021

Magpie River

Magpie River — Innu Council & Minganie, Québec — nine recognized rights.

2025

Colorado River

Colorado River Indian Tribes — personhood under Tribal law along 100 river miles.

NEXT

South Platte River

Initiative 02 — Turquoise Necklace Cultural Plan

Sixteen parks. One living corridor of renewal, healing, and connection.

The Turquoise Necklace envisions a connected corridor of 16 parks and open spaces along the South Platte — each a “bead” carrying cultural storytelling, public art, and community-centered design. The Congreso proposes renaming Vanderbilt Park East to Seven Generations Park, honoring a shared responsibility to the river and to those yet to come. Explore the beads below.

The framework

Eight Guiding Principles

The historic and cultural contributions of Indigenous, Chicano, and Mexicano/Latino peoples are the foundational roots of the Congreso's mission, threaded through every principle.

I

Identity

Representing and promoting the cultural diversity that gives the river's communities their unique identity.

II

Respect

Stewarding the river, its ecosystem, culture, and history with reverence for their intrinsic value.

III

Rescue

Building the Necklace with strategies that recover and revitalize the river and its surroundings.

IV

Social Cohesion

Empowering marginalized communities with equitable access to the corridor's opportunities.

V

Responsibility

Designing the Necklace as the shared duty of every resident of the South Platte basin.

VI

Economic Development

Promoting sustainable prosperity that benefits river communities while preserving cultural and environmental integrity.

VII

Resiliency

Strengthening the river and its communities against environmental, social, and economic challenges.

VIII

Inclusion

Embracing the interconnection of culture, spirit, water, and land in one harmonious community.

Call to action

Denver can become the national model for a city that honors its river.

The Congreso calls on Denver City Council to recognize the South Platte as a living relative, integrate cultural guardianship into river planning, and embed environmental equity into municipal policy — endorsing the River Personhood Framework and the Turquoise Necklace as city-recognized initiatives.
